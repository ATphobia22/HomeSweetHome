About this world

Mount Vernon, Indiana, rebuilt as a living open world from public data. Nothing here is a simulation except the flood slider — and the slider is only a water level, not a forecast.

What’s real

  • Ground: Indiana 2020 DEM mosaic (33 tiles, USGS 3DEP / IndianaMap), 2.5 ft pixels, resampled to a 200×200 grid. Vertical datum treated as NAVD88 (assumed from source fields, not independently certified).
  • Buildings: 4,398 LOD1 prisms from IGIO Building Footprints of Indiana 2016–2020 (LiDAR 2020). Heights are derived estimates (Overture Maps nearest match where available, 4 m rural median otherwise) — not surveyed, not certified, not regulatory.
  • Streets: 1,109 OpenStreetMap ways (Geofabrik Indiana, 2026-11-18, ODbL). Names shown only where OSM has them.
  • Aerial photo: USDA NAIP, public domain, four quadrants mosaicked for this area. The live USDA fetch failed from the build machine, so the 2026-11-19 composite is reused; its extent is the same area as the elevation grid.
  • Landmarks: descriptions from the National Register form for the Mount Vernon Downtown Historic District (NPS Form 10-900, 2003). Positions from OSM points where available; street-address positions are approximate and labelled as such.
  • People: Oak Grove Slim is the hero. Cutouts are the real family photos, used as still figures — nobody is animated.

What’s estimated or interpretive

  • Building heights (see above) and the courthouse, gazebo and amphitheater models, which are built from written descriptions, not measured drawings.
  • Downtown storefront positions from street addresses on the town grid — approximate.
  • The riverfront amphitheater is an interpretive addition for the open world.
  • Tree placement is seeded scatter on open ground, not a tree survey.
  • South of the river, the elevation grid has an inpainted no-data patch (Kentucky bottomland) — flat by construction, photo texture still shows the fields.

The flood slider

The slider raises a water plane from normal pool (342.5 ft) to 375 ft. The marked line is the base flood elevation, 369.1 ft NAVD88 (FEMA FIS 18129CV000A Table 9). The count of buildings below the waterline is a simple ground-elevation count, not a damage estimate and not a regulatory determination. The 1937 crest — 59.25 ft on the Mount Vernon gauge, February 2, 1937 — is a gauge height, not a NAVD88 elevation.
